Abstract

Molecular design agents choose interventions by scoring predicted outcomes, which requires the scorer to distinguish the available actions. We test this requirement using 78 COSMIC mutational processes as actions and distance in ESM-2 representation space as the score. Median pairwise distance falls from 0.815 in COSMIC mutational-spectrum space to 0.0068 between ESM-2 outcome regions, a 120× compression, and a composition-matched control reproduces the destinations. A learned policy identifies the true action in only 7.55% of held-out cases, while split-conformal prediction reaches 92.2% coverage only by returning 66.8 of 78 actions on average. At 100 scorer calls, adaptive action selection reaches mean target distance 0.177, compared with 0.165 for a fixed policy and 0.173 for a random-action policy, and an independent Pfam profile hidden Markov model finds no PF17041 hits at the curated threshold. These results separate biological admissibility from scorer-level action identifiability and show why molecular agents should test whether their scorer distinguishes candidate actions before reporting a specific mechanism.

The requirement nobody checks 🔍

A closed-loop molecular agent proposes an intervention, scores the resulting molecule, and uses the score to choose what to do next. This searches large spaces efficiently, but only if the scorer resolves the actions available to the policy. If several interventions produce indistinguishable scored outcomes, the agent cannot infer which intervention caused a result — and any mechanism it reports afterwards is a guess wearing the clothes of an inference.

Action identifiability

The ability to distinguish the outcomes of competing actions using the agent's own scoring model.

Identifiability is not the same as biological admissibility. An action may represent a perfectly valid biological process even when the scorer discards exactly the information that distinguishes it from other processes. The two properties come apart, and only one of them is usually checked.

Key contributions

  • We name and operationalize action identifiability as a precondition for closed-loop molecular design, and give a cheap diagnostic for it: compare distances in the action's own parameterization against distances between scored outcomes.
  • We build a controlled testbed where ground-truth distinctions between actions are known independently of any learned model, using the 78 COSMIC single-base-substitution signatures as the action space.
  • We show the requirement fails for a standard action–scorer pair, with a 120× compression of the action space and a policy that names the right action 7.55% of the time.
  • We show matched controls erase the apparent gains — composition matching, edit-count matching, unselected drift, and equal scorer-call budgets each remove a result we would otherwise have reported.
  • We propose a three-step evaluation that any molecular agent paper can run before claiming a mechanism.

The testbed 🧬

We need an action space where the ground-truth distinctions are known independently of any learned model. COSMIC single-base-substitution signatures give us exactly that: each signature is a distribution over 96 trinucleotide substitution types, catalogued from real mutational processes, with its own model-free parameterization.

An action is a signature s, represented by a distribution over the 96 substitution types. Executing s on a coding sequence samples compatible substitutions and produces a mutant, which we translate, embed with ESM-2, and score by cosine distance to a target Pfam-domain centroid — lower is better. This gives both halves of the comparison: how far apart two actions are as processes, and how far apart their outcomes are after scoring.

The score measures movement in representation space. It does not directly measure protein function or acquisition of the target domain — a distinction that turns out to matter a great deal.

The scorer compresses the action space 📉

Across all 3,003 pairs of COSMIC signatures, the median distance between native 96-channel spectra is 0.815. The median distance between the same actions' ESM-2 outcome regions is 0.0068. The scored action space is roughly two orders of magnitude tighter than the space the actions actually live in.

1,780 of 3,003 pairs (59.3%) are more than 0.5 apart in COSMIC space but fall within 0.011 of each other after scoring. SBS1 and SBS7a — clock-like ageing and UV exposure, about as biologically distinct as two processes get — are 0.952 apart as spectra and 0.0055 apart as outcomes.

A composition-matched control pins down why. Scrambling trinucleotide context and mutation placement while preserving the realized substitution composition reproduces each action's ESM-2 destination to within cosine distance 0.02. Actions with similar broad substitution patterns collapse together, and the same pattern appears at both ESM-2 8M and 650M. The agent has 78 nominal actions and a far coarser effective action space underneath.

Matched controls remove the gain ⚖️

On the PF17041 target, the structured action looks like it works. It reduces ESM-2 distance by 0.0736 ± 0.0030 across five seeds, against 0.0218 ± 0.0051 for edit-count-matched random mutation — an improvement of 0.0519, 95% CI [0.0477, 0.0573]. That is the result a design paper would report and stop.

Then composition matching erases it. Reapplying the same amino-acid substitution multiset at random positions produces 0.0764, against 0.0736 for the structured action.

The paired difference between the structured action and its composition-matched control is −0.0027, 95% CI [−0.0063, 0.0012]. We find no evidence that trinucleotide context or mutation placement — the things that actually define a mutational signature — add steering power beyond the substitution composition ESM-2 already preserves.

Scorer feedback adds nothing either. An unselected drift control scores every proposal and then accepts it regardless of the score. It improves target distance by 0.0737, against 0.0736 under Metropolis selection; for the random arm, 0.0236 unselected against 0.0218 selected. Whatever the loop is getting, it is getting from the proposal distribution, not from the feedback.

Adaptive selection does not pay for itself ⚡

Giving the agent more candidates to evaluate does not rescue it. At a budget of 100 ESM-2 scorer calls, five-action one-step lookahead reaches a mean target distance of 0.177, worse than both baselines.

Adaptive is worse than the fixed policy by +0.0113, 95% CI [+0.0082, +0.0143], and worse than random by +0.0042, 95% CI [+0.0021, +0.0057]. Lookahead spends scorer calls on comparisons the scorer cannot resolve, and pays for them in lost transitions. Evaluating more candidate actions does not improve optimization when the feedback carries too little usable information.

The policy cannot name the action 🎯

We trained a supervised policy on 35,403 realized Pfam-domain transitions to predict which of the 78 COSMIC signatures produced a given transition. On a protein-grouped split it reaches 7.55% top-1 accuracy, against 6.12% for a nearest-neighbor baseline and 1.28% for uniform chance. Measured against a train-only per-(origin, target) frequency lookup, the policy's edge is +0.93 percentage points, 95% CI [+0.71, +1.27].

So the policy does carry real information about action choice — and it still predicts the wrong action in 92.45% of held-out cases. It also fails to generalize: holding out complete COSMIC processes drops top-1 to 0.8%, below the 1.3% uniform baseline, and holding out SBS17a yields 0% recovery. The model interpolates among action classes it saw during training rather than learning what a signature is.

Set-valued prediction buys coverage by giving up specificity. Adaptive prediction sets reach 92.2% coverage but return 66.8 of 78 actions on average, with a median set size of 78 — the entire catalogue. A direct ESM-2 neighborhood reaches 82.6% coverage at a mean set size of 58.2. Abstention produces smaller outputs, but retaining 8.6% of test cases still yields only 33.0% conditional accuracy.

This is what a non-identifiable action space looks like from the inside. The uncertainty quantification is not malfunctioning — it is correctly reporting that the evidence does not single out an action.

What the representation can decode

The representation is not empty, and it is worth being precise about that. A probe that reads an ESM-2 displacement and names which of five processes produced it reaches 66% five-way accuracy against 20% chance, and 89% on SBS17a. The structure of its errors is the finding: SBS1 is confused with SBS2 even though their COSMIC spectra are near-orthogonal at cosine 0.02. The representation decodes a real signal and still merges processes the catalogue separates. Decoding is not identifiability.

Moving in the objective is not acquiring the domain 🧪

The optimization moves proteins measurably closer to the PF17041 centroid. The obvious question is whether that means anything biologically. We checked with a model that shares nothing with the objective: an independent Pfam profile hidden Markov model, evaluated with HMMER 3 against the PF17041.11 SasG E profile.

Zero evaluated sequences passed Pfam's curated gathering threshold. Under a permissive reporting threshold, none of the five structured-action sequences produces a match at all; other arms produce only weak, non-significant hits, with best E = 0.34.

The objective is also exploitable, and the exploit is instructive. An agent making unconstrained single-residue edits drives cosine distance to the PF17041 centroid from 0.26 to 0.009 over 200 edits, landing 5.0 standard deviations below the natural-protein reference mean of 0.194 ± 0.037 across 25 measured proteins. ESM-2 naturalness falls alongside it, from −0.41 to −0.50. The plausibility signal does detect the departure — but because both the objective and that self-check live in ESM-2, passing a check in the same representation is not independent evidence.

The evidence supports movement under the ESM-2 objective. It does not support acquisition of PF17041-like sequence properties. These are different claims, and only the first is measured by the thing the agent optimizes.

The question the analysis was built to settle

"""Is the action space degenerate, or does the scorer collapse it?

Three biochemically distinct actions (SBS1, SBS7a, SBS2) drive proteins to nearly
one destination in ESM-2 space. That has a confound four signatures cannot settle:
the actions might genuinely be similar, or the SCORER might be unable to resolve
differences that exist.

This settles it by measuring all 78 actions in BOTH spaces:

  native space : the COSMIC 96-channel trinucleotide substitution profile, which is
                 the action's own parameterization, independent of any learned model
  scorer space : the mean final-layer ESM-2 position its mutants reach

If the catalogue is well separated natively and compressed in scorer space, the
collapse belongs to the scorer.
"""

What it adds up to

Distinct actions become indistinguishable

Biological grounding does not guarantee scorer-level identifiability. COSMIC describes genuinely distinct processes; ESM-2 maps many of their outcomes to nearly the same place.

Agency does not overcome poor resolution

Adaptive selection, lookahead, and uncertainty quantification cannot recover a distinction the scorer has already thrown away.

Optimization requires independent validation

Movement under an objective is not acquisition of the property the objective stands in for, and a check sharing the objective's representation cannot tell you otherwise.

A three-step evaluation we would like to see become standard

  1. Measure whether the scorer distinguishes the actions available to the policy. Compare distances in the action's own parameterization against distances between scored outcomes. This is cheap, and it is diagnostic.
  2. Compare optimization against controls matched for proposal distribution, edit count, sequence length, and scorer-call budget. Each of these matched controls removed a result we would otherwise have reported.
  3. Validate the molecular claim with a measurement independent of the optimization objective. If the objective and the check share a representation, passing the check is not evidence.

If the scorer cannot resolve competing actions, the right behavior is to return a useful uncertainty set or abstain — not to name a mechanism.

Scope and limitations. These conclusions apply to the action–scorer pair studied here; other molecular actions or scorers may preserve more mechanistic information, and the point of the recipe is that this is a measurable question rather than an assumption. The steering and decoding experiments cover a subset of signatures while the destination analysis covers all 78, so catalogue-wide overlap is established and per-signature steering specificity is not. ESM-2 8M and 650M share training data and tokenization, so the scale comparison is not an independent-model replication. The 0.011 collapse bar is inherited from an earlier four-signature experiment and does not carry identical meaning across every model-by-readout condition, so the catalogue-wide conclusions rest on continuous distances rather than on that threshold. The unconstrained-optimization result shows this objective can be exploited under single-residue search, not that every constrained design objective fails the same way.

Dual use. Methods that steer protein representations are dual-use. We restrict the action space to characterized processes and focus on auditing failure modes rather than claiming successful molecular design.
